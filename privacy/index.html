Naila’s Orbit Privacy Policy

Last updated: 10 November 2026

Naila’s Orbit is a game for iPhone made by Abbas Asadov. This policy covers the version of Naila’s Orbit on the App Store.

In short: the game has no account and no server of its own, and it does not collect your data itself. It shows short ads from Google AdMob after some wins and after many lost tries, and Google's software collects some device and usage data to show them; the game never asks to track you. This version sells nothing: there are no in-app purchases.

What the game stores, and where

  • Your progress (cleared levels, seeds, best times, medals, Glowspores and light trails), your settings (language, sound, vibration, lantern color), the ads' own counts (sessions, ads shown, your age group) and, for the optional reminders, your choice and the hours at which you last opened the game are stored only on your iPhone, in the game's own storage, and in your own iCloud or computer backups if you use them. They are never sent to me. If you use Game Center, your achievement progress and Thorn trial times also go to Apple (see below).
  • Deleting the game deletes this data. Settings → Reset progress erases your progress (your settings, Glowspores and trails and the age answer are kept).

Ads (Google AdMob)

The game is free and shows ads made by Google AdMob (Google LLC). The rule is built into the game:

  • Ad breaks come at two calm moments: after a level was won (once its flower has bloomed, before the result screen), and after many lost tries, when the level first pauses and an "Ad break…" notice holds the screen for a second, so no tap meant for the game lands on the ad; then a Try again card, the game never goes on by itself. Pause → Restart counts as a lost try, so a break that is due can follow it, with the same notice. Google decides when each ad can be closed, usually after a few seconds. No banners, no ad when the game opens.
  • Your first minutes are ad-free: ads begin once you have played about five minutes and won five levels. A player who never gets that far never meets Google's ad software.
  • These breaks are at least 2 to 4 minutes of play apart (the first garden is the gentlest), and a break after lost tries needs many of them: 12 in the first garden, 8 in the second, 6 later. Never in the daily garden, never in the middle of play, never right after the rating prompt.
  • Rewarded ads come only when you choose them: when a level has beaten you several times, Skip can offer one short ad to skip it (two for a garden's final scene), and a garden gate's Pass through two. The card says the cost before any ad, saying no costs nothing (after a declined Skip the next ad break even waits a little longer), after more tries they are free, and players under 13 are never offered one.
  • Every ad is rated "G" (general audience) at most.

What Google collects. When ads are on, Google's Mobile Ads SDK and its consent SDK (User Messaging Platform) collect, according to their own privacy declarations: a device identifier, the general location derived from your IP address, information about the ads shown to you and how you interacted with them, how the app is used, and crash and performance data. Google uses this to show, limit and measure ads, to prevent fraud and to improve its software. The game does not read this data and does not receive it. Google's policy: https://policies.google.com/privacy, and how Google uses data from apps that use its services: https://policies.google.com/technologies/partner-sites.

No tracking. The game never asks for permission to track you (Apple's App Tracking Transparency), so your advertising identifier (IDFA) stays unavailable to the game and to Google. Google's "publisher first-party ID" is turned off in the game.

Your age. When the ads begin, the game asks once, neutrally, for your birth year. Only an age group is kept on your iPhone (under 13, 13 to 17, 18 or older), never the year, and it is never asked again. Because only the year is known, a birth year that could fall in two groups counts in the younger one. For players under 13, the game turns on Google's child treatment: no personalized ads, no remarketing, no advertising identifier and no third-party ad vendors. For 13 to 17, Google's teen treatment: no personalized ads. Naila’s Orbit is not part of the App Store's Kids category.

Consent (EEA, UK, Switzerland). Where the law asks for it, Google's consent form appears before any ad is requested, and you can change your choice any time in Settings → About → Privacy choices. Without consent Google shows only limited, non-personalized ads or none.

Report an ad. Each ad carries Google's ad options (the small icon in a corner), where you can report it to Google. You can also write to me (nailasorbit.help@gmail.com, or Settings → Ads → Report an ad): tell me when you saw it and what it showed, and I will block it in AdMob.

Game Center (optional)

If you are signed in to Game Center on your iPhone, the game reports your achievement progress and your Thorn trial clear times to Apple's Game Center. Apple keeps them and shows them to you and, on the leaderboards, to other players under your Game Center nickname. This is Apple's service, covered by Apple's privacy policy. The game sends none of it to me. In App Store Connect I can see a leaderboard's top entries (nickname and time) and remove cheated scores. To play without Game Center, decline the sign-in or sign out in the iPhone's Settings → Game Center.

Reminders (optional)

After the first level you win, the game asks once, through the iPhone's own question, whether it may send you notifications. If you agree, it sets a few silent reminders to return to your garden for the days you stay away: at most two a week, never at night. The iPhone itself shows them: the game has no server and no push service, and nothing is sent anywhere. Turn them off any time in the game's Settings → Reminders or in the iPhone's Settings → Notifications. If you decline, nothing changes in the game.

Information Apple shares with developers

Apple may share anonymous, aggregated statistics with me, such as downloads, crash reports and usage, only from people who chose to share them with app developers. This is governed by Apple's privacy policy, and I cannot identify individual players from it. AdMob shows me only totals (ads shown, earnings by country), never data about a single player.

What the game does not do

  • No account and no sign-in of its own (Game Center is optional).
  • No analytics of its own and no server of mine. Without a connection the game plays fully; only the ads (and Game Center) need the internet.
  • No camera, microphone, contacts, photos or location, and no tracking prompt. The only permission the game asks for is notifications, for the optional reminders.

Test versions (TestFlight)

Test builds distributed through TestFlight show only Google's test ads and also keep a play log on the device: levels started, cleared and lost, attempts, hits and falls, when the hint hand appeared, whether the opening was watched or skipped, time played, the days the game was opened, the phone model and iOS version, and for the ads: whether an ad was shown or skipped (and why), Google's consent answer, the age group (under 13 or not), No Ads offers, purchases and restores. With it go iOS's own reports (MetricKit) of the game's crashes, hangs and launch times: the game's code positions and timings, the phone model, iOS version and region format. The log holds no name, email or contacts, is excluded from backups and is never sent automatically. A tester can share it by hand from Settings → Test log. Logs that testers send me are used only to improve the game and are deleted when that work is done. App Store versions keep no play log and no MetricKit reports and show Google's regular ads; so does the final App Store build when it is installed through TestFlight before release. Apple's TestFlight terms also apply.

Children

Naila’s Orbit is a game for everyone, including children. The game itself collects no data from anyone. For players under 13 (by the age question), Google's ads run with Google's child treatment described above, and all ads are rated "G" at most. This version sells nothing, so no one sees a purchase prompt.

Keeping and deleting data

The game keeps its data only on your iPhone (Game Center's data stays with Apple, under your Game Center account); to remove the game's data, delete the game. Google keeps ad data under its own policy; you can reset or limit it in Google's ad settings and, in the EEA, UK and Switzerland, in Settings → About → Privacy choices. If you sent me a TestFlight play log, write to me and I will delete it.

Changes

If the game changes how it handles data, this policy and the App Store privacy information will be updated before that version is released.
